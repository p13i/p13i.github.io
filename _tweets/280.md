> ["The Voice of Hind Rajab" director] Ben Hania has said of
> the project: “The heart of this film is something very
> simple, and very hard to live with. I cannot accept a
> world where a child calls for help and no one comes. That
> pain, that failure, belongs to all of us. This story is
> not just about Gaza. It speaks to a universal grief. I
> believe that fiction (especially when it draws from
> verified, painful, real events) is cinema’s most powerful
> tool. More powerful than the noise of breaking news or the
> forgetfulness of scrolling. Cinema can preserve a memory.
> Cinema can resist amnesia.”
